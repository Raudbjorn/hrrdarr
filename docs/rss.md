# Durable RSS and submission receipts

The native RSS consumer fetches Torznab/Newznab release facts, evaluates the current TV or movie library policy, persists delayed decisions, and submits accepted torrent releases through qBittorrent. It uses the existing single local command worker, provider transport limits and database ownership lock. A feed fetch alone is not a completed RSS pass.

`POST /api/v1/rss/commands` takes `{target:{media_type,indexer_id,indexer_revision,client_selection},priority}`. Client selection is either `{"kind":"default"}` or `{"kind":"explicit","provider_id":"UUID","provider_revision":1}`. The older `client_id` plus `client_revision` pair remains supported only when `client_selection` is omitted; mixed, incomplete and null forms are rejected. Existing commands, schedules and receipts retain `legacy_fixed` semantics after migration. Responses expose `selection_kind` and nullable client fields; default commands and schedules remain unresolved until each release matches its library owner.

Default selection first finds matching scoped client eligibility tags. If any enabled client's tags match the series/movie tags, matching clients replace the untagged pool; otherwise only untagged clients qualify. The indexer's non-null `download_client_id` then restricts that pool. An unavailable preferred client cannot silently fall through to another client. Explicit selection overrides tags, binding, backoff preference and rotation, while retaining enabled-state, protocol, domain and revision checks. Legacy fixed-pair requests continue to enforce their original binding rules. Indexer revisions and operation flags remain authoritative for all intents.

Unblocked clients are preferred even over blocked clients with better priority. Ordinary new sends may try when all eligible clients are blocked; a persisted `download_client_unavailable` decision switches to strict filtering and checks again after 60 seconds. Unbound movie defaults select one UUID from the best-priority group, advancing their process-local cursor only after committed selection; bound movie choices do not advance that cursor. TV defaults order priority groups and rotate after the last successful default client, including a bound choice. Cursors are separate by domain and reset on restart; captured candidate plans do not change on restart. Plans and the total attempt history are bounded to 128 entries; overflow fails visibly rather than truncating eligibility.

Commands share the global 1,024-row admission cap and priority ordering with download refresh, metadata refresh and blocklist clear. One active RSS command is admitted per indexer, selection intent, fixed client where supplied, and domain. `GET /rss/commands` supports the existing bounded command query; detail, cancellation and terminal-history deletion use `/rss/commands/{id}` and `/cancel`.

`GET /rss/candidates` returns paginated receipts with optional `media_type`, `command_id` and closed `status` filters (limit 1–100, offset at most 10,000). Responses contain safe titles, typed targets, decision reasons, timestamps, errors and ordered client attempts (UUID, revision, state and static error only). The candidate client fields identify its active attempt and eventual winning client. Locators, GUIDs, attributes, credentials and prepared transport payloads are private. `DELETE /rss/candidates/{id}` removes only unclaimed rejected/cancelled receipts. Receipt deletion does not remove a remote download.

`GET/POST /rss/schedules` manages at most 64 persisted schedules. Intervals are 60–86,400 seconds. Creates omit `revision`; updates supply the exact returned revision. `DELETE /rss/schedules/{id}?revision=N` also fences concurrent changes. Provider edits disable stale schedules visibly; provider deletion removes its schedules but retains command and submission history. Missed intervals coalesce into one due run. Admission failures are recorded and the next due time advances.

All paths above have the `/api/v1` prefix. Request bodies are at most 8 KiB, responses at most 1 MiB, and API I/O has a five-second deadline. A timeout is an unknown request outcome: read back commands/schedules before deciding whether to issue another request.

Release-profile admission uses each receipt's own indexer, including candidates from another command in the same pending cohort. All applicable profiles are enforced again under the writer before preparation and after network reads. Operation-owned exact term evidence survives global-cache eviction for the complete bounded cohort; current applicability and temporal fields are never cached as an admission decision. Transient matcher/storage errors preserve private pending state for retry. A restrictive edit after preparation prevents submission; observed/submitting/reconciling ownership remains governed by existing recovery rules. See [release profiles](release-profiles.md) for semantics and memory/concurrency limits.

## Execution and recovery

Each pass captures at most 1,000 releases across ten pages, with a 4 MiB aggregate private encoding limit and a 35-second capture deadline. Malformed-page/item warnings currently fail the pass visibly. The entire feed capture and decision checkpoint commits together; retries reuse that checkpoint. Rejected or cancelled, unclaimed releases may be evaluated again on a later feed pass as availability, monitoring or policy changes. Existing owned submissions are never replaced by that reevaluation.

Pending payloads use bounded AES-GCM envelopes with a separate purpose and candidate/provider/revision/domain associated data. A configured provider key is required; there is no plaintext fallback. Each envelope is at most 65,565 bytes, all envelopes together at most 16 MiB, and receipts at most 1,024 rows. Rejection/cancellation releases encrypted payload storage. Lost keys cause a visible failure, never a new submission.

A delayed candidate retains its typed target. When due, the evaluator checks current policy again. A changed target is rejected rather than silently retargeted. Immediately after read-only preparation, an immediate transaction rechecks current local policy, provider revisions and target/hash ownership. It records the immutable safe submission identity and claims every prepared torrent hash before transitioning to `submitting`. Only then may the network mutation run. No database transaction spans a network call.

Migration 0052 journals each selected/prepared/dispatching client attempt with a global ordinal and selection round. Only unbound default TV selection may advance to the next captured client after a classified result proves no add was dispatched. That safe failure and release of its hash claims commit together before another client can acquire authority. Movies, explicit requests and indexer-bound defaults have a single-client plan. The first successful preparation freezes the actual release-byte hash across every subsequent attempt and round; changed fetched content rejects fallback. Encrypted release locators survive safe alternatives for at most 30 minutes from the first captured client plan. A later round never extends that deadline.

Default RSS and automatic-search candidates retain their encrypted release and original target when an entire TV plan safely fails, including a bound singleton. They wait at least 60 seconds before capturing a new plan from current strictly eligible clients. Movie candidates acquire this retry authority only for a typed client authentication or availability failure; a generic HTTP 503 is not enough. A client can appear once per round and again in a later round, with a maximum of 128 attempts across the candidate's lifetime. No eligible client postpones the check without creating a round or extending the payload expiry. Interactive grabs, explicit choices and legacy fixed requests do not acquire this new automatic retry authority.

An unavailable result also parks later qualified torrent releases in the same RSS command before they attempt a client. Existing policy delays, rejected decisions, other commands and already-started submissions retain their own state. This batch decision is persisted across worker ticks and restart. Local capacity/cancellation and release-fetch or parsing failures do not manufacture client-unavailable retries.

A local provider database read failure is distinct from a release or client failure. Preparation retains any durably recorded byte identity and pending payload. If submission positively proves that no add occurred, a `local_storage_retry` checkpoint retains the same client, attempt ordinal, round, identity and original expiry, with a future retry deadline. It does not select an alternate, start a new round or penalize client health. The safe checkpoint and claim release commit together; failure to publish them leaves the original dispatch journal for conservative recovery. No send occurs before identity persistence succeeds. Bytes fetched but never successfully written to the journal are not crash-persistent evidence.

After an add may have been dispatched, the boundary is irrevocable. Timeout, disconnect, cancellation after dispatch, follow-up option failure or restart permits only read-only reconciliation against the original client, at most three observations. Private release payloads are erased when uncertainty settles; immutable attempts and hash ownership remain. `NotObserved` never authorizes another POST. Presence discovered during reconciliation becomes `needs_attention/presence_unconfirmed`: it cannot establish that this process submitted the torrent or completed its configured options. An already-present torrent becomes `needs_attention/preexisting_download`, without a trusted association. Only a direct successful submitted-and-observed outcome becomes `observed`. The receipt and its typed target form the native grab history for this unit; this is not an import event.

Client/hash ownership and typed target ownership prevent another release from acquiring the same target, even through another client. Owned receipts and hash claims are retained. Completed owned imports release the corresponding target-admission fence through validated import provenance; retained receipts and hash claims remain durable. Uncertainty evidence is not automatically deleted to free capacity.

Pending RSS offers for the same movie, or the same series with overlapping episode IDs, form a bounded group across command IDs. Selection considers the complete group rather than a truncated subset. Oldest pending publication can remove a full-profile wait even when that older offer is currently ineligible for selection. Its age must be strictly greater than the configured delay; an offer's own age may equal the delay. Cancelled, prepared and unrelated-target receipts do not supply pending publication facts. Explicit-search receipts retain their own decision authority.

Selection and all local admission writes reread policy inside an immediate transaction. A newly imposed delay preserves a pending receipt's encrypted payload and fresh deadline, including after network preparation. Once a receipt is prepared, its continuation takes precedence within the overlapping group and keeps its original command/provider/origin. Current policy, source and identity are still checked: a newly imposed delay at this prepared stage retains the existing `target_changed` rejection behavior; pending preservation is not claimed for that stage. Submitting and reconciling ownership is unchanged.

Permanent malformed sibling errors settle only that sibling. Exact CF state/admission/worker errors and policy/SQL storage-read failures abort local settlement without deleting pending payloads or overwriting deadlines. Standalone due work remains eligible for the existing worker retry tick; parent-command/capture errors retain their bounded command retry behavior. Static error codes contain no decrypted release facts.

Cancellation stops work before dispatch. It cannot undo a dispatched torrent; existing uncertain receipts continue read-only reconciliation. Local storage failures after dispatch return to recovery without resubmitting. Due delayed/reconciliation work joins the existing priority/creation ordering, so a stream of newer normal commands cannot indefinitely defer older receipts.

Command `succeeded` means the current feed/decision pass ended, **not** that downloads completed. Delayed candidates can later fail while command status remains terminal. Command progress counters read current receipt states; candidate receipts are authoritative for later outcomes. Uncertain immediate submissions fail their pass visibly. Queue association is populated only from an exact client UUID, revision and validated returned remote torrent ID with a trusted `observed` receipt. That remote ID can differ from the prepared v1/v2 hashes; it commits atomically with the observed status while all original hash ownership claims remain retained. Unknown, pre-existing and uncertain downloads remain unassociated.

## Scope and evidence limits

This consumer dispatches single-episode TV releases and movies. Packs are parsed but rejected explicitly before preparation; they are not reduced to an arbitrary episode. Usenet cannot be sent to qBittorrent. Unequal recent/older client priorities are explicitly unsupported until the domain-specific recent-release policy is implemented. The evaluator exposes other unsupported policies as reasons rather than accepting them silently.

The native [completed-download import](download-processing.md) path is delivered with scoped recovery evidence. Unified History grabbed events, full failed-download handling and automatic removal, release breadth, additional clients and full upstream RSS parity remain incomplete. `observed` does not prove download completion, import success, filesystem layout or recovery of every follow-up option. No live service equivalence is claimed.

Runnable regressions are in [rss_commands.rs](../tests/rss_commands.rs); provider protocol coverage remains in [qbittorrent_protocol.rs](../tests/qbittorrent_protocol.rs). Fixtures use scratch databases and owned loopback servers.

Focused consumer evidence includes `pending_cohort_crosses_commands_and_attributes_sibling_errors` (both domains, strict boundary, membership, sibling attribution, actual unavailable-table retries), plus the real worker tests `pending_cross_command_winner_keeps_provider_and_submits_once`, `local_policy_rechecked_after_preparation_and_postsubmit_failure_recovers`, and `prepared_owner_still_rechecks_increased_delay_before_post`. All use scratch databases and owned ephemeral listeners. Protocol ranking evidence does not prove an end-to-end Usenet client submission.
